The Vocabulary Guide

Words are the raw material of any language. Learn the right ones, in the right way, and every other skill gets easier.

Start with the most frequent words

A relatively small set of words makes up most of what people say. Learning the 1,000 most common words first gives you the biggest return on your time. Frequency lists and graded readers are built around this idea.

Learn words in context

Instead of a single word, learn a short phrase or example sentence: not just “tired” but “I’m a bit tired today”. Context shows you how the word is used, which words go with it and how it sounds in real speech.

Flashcard apps that use spaced repetition handle the timing for you.

From knowing to using

  1. Recognise: you understand it when you see or hear it.
  2. Recall: you can remember it when you need it.
  3. Use: you say or write it naturally.

Move words along this path by writing your own sentences and using new words in conversation the same week.

How many words a day?

Five to fifteen new words a day is sustainable for most adults. More than that often creates a review pile you cannot keep up with. The Vocabulary Goal Planner shows how your daily number adds up.

Memory tricks that help

  • Link a new word to a vivid image or a similar-sounding word you know.
  • Group words by topic: kitchen, travel, work.
  • Say words aloud as you review them.
  • Label things around your home with sticky notes.
